m3llo
Design system · v3.3 · graphite · red mark

Red is state.
White is action.
Everything else is graphite.

m3llo is graphite, white and one red. The red is the 3 in the mark, and it is the only colour allowed to say this is happening now. White carries every action. Everything else is greyscale, so the only saturated things on screen are the game footage and the brand.

01

The colour rule

Four tiers. The split that keeps two accents from fighting: red is state, white is action. Red tells you something is happening; white is what you press to do something about it. They never mean the same thing, so they can sit on the same screen without competing.

RedThe mark, and now
#FF453A — two jobs, no others. One: the 3 in m3llo, everywhere the mark appears. Two: this is happening — live badges, the live-now kicker, recording and clip marks, the unread dot, leave call. Red is a state, never a surface and never decoration.
WhiteThe action
#FFFFFF — the thing you press: the primary button, the active channel you are sitting in, a focus ring, the big number. White is loud enough on graphite that it never needs help. One filled white button per surface.
Red againThe commit
#FF453A — the button that commits wears the mark’s red. Create the crew, join it, save the settings, sign in, start the stream from the picker. This is the second and last place red appears as a fill, and the reason is the same as the first: it marks the moment something becomes true. White stays the colour of every other thing you can press — STREAM included, because STREAM opens a choice rather than settling one.
GreenSpeaking, and only that
#3FD07A — the person talking right now, on their name in the voice list. Green appears nowhere else in the product. White was too quiet to carry it and red is taken. The avatar does not change, and neither does the row.
IdentityPeople, not chrome
Five muted hues for avatars, crew marks and game tiles. They are desaturated on purpose — enough to tell Ostkatt from Propotato at a glance, not enough to read as interface colour. Never on a button, a border, or a label.
Over footageIts own ground
A chip, a scrubber or a play disc laid on a game frame cannot borrow the graphite ramp, because the frame underneath is any colour at all. They get black washes and white text: #00000044 at rest, #000000AA for a chip, #00000066 for a bar, #FFFFFF33 for a track. These are washes, not palette.
ContentUntouched
Game footage keeps its full colour. Thumbnails, streams and clips are the only saturated things in the product. A very slight saturate(.85) keeps them from screaming, and nothing else is applied.

The test: take a screenshot and desaturate everything except the media and the mark. Only two things should lose colour — a live badge and a record dot — and both of them are red because something is genuinely happening. If anything else changes, it was decoration.

03

Typography

Two faces, and one question: does this name something, or is it big? If yes, Oxanium. If no, Barlow. Barlow then works in two registers — 300 for sentences, 600 with wide tracking for micro labels — which is what lets a single text face carry both the chat panel and the instrument chrome. Nothing is italic; nothing is uppercase except the label register.

Barlow has proportional figures by default, so any column of numbers wobbles between rows. Every place digits stack — records, durations, counts, the recap ledger — sets font-variant-numeric:tabular-nums. This is not optional; it is the one thing a mono face gave us for free.

Shape

Unchanged from v2, and the one part of the system that carries personality now that colour does not. A clipped keyline layer, a clipped fill inset by 1px, and one corner cut away.

Two or three cards per screen take a corner other than the default. Deliberate, not alternating.

Avatars sit in a row with a gap. They never overlap. A stack is borrowed from photo avatars, where a face survives being half covered. Initials do not: clip "xt" and it reads "t". A cut corner over a neighbour also reads as a bite taken out of it.

05

Components

Buttons

White filled is primary, one per surface. Red filled is the commit — the button that creates, joins, saves or signs in. A dialog has exactly one, and it sits bottom-right. White outline is a second white-worthy action on the same screen. Leave is filled red too, because ending the call commits as much as starting the crew does. Clip outlines in red rather than filling: it describes a state it is about to create, and it sits next to nothing else red.

Fields

A field is a plain rectangle. No radius and no cut: the cut belongs to things you press and things that hold content, and a field is neither — it is a hole you type into. Square corners also keep a stack of fields reading as one block instead of a row of pills. Dots, discs and progress tracks keep their radius; nothing else does.

Before shipping a screen: count the white fills — one button. Then read every red thing out loud. If you cannot finish the sentence "this is happening right now", or it is not the 3 in the mark, it should not be red.
